## Begin with a fictional capitalization table

Two founders own 600 and 400 issued shares in this original arithmetic exercise. There are no options, debt, preference rights or other securities. The total is 1,000, so their simple issued-share fractions are 60% and 40%. Those fractions answer the declared share-count question. They do not establish voting control, cash value, liquidation proceeds or the terms of a real company.

## Model a new issue separately from a transfer

Assume the company issues 250 identical shares for 5,000 units of cash. The total becomes 1,250: founders hold 48% and 32%, and the investor holds 20%. The company receives the cash in this fictional primary issuance. If a founder instead sells existing shares to another holder, the total need not change and the selling holder receives that price under the exercise’s assumption. Keep these two transactions separate.

## Separate valuation arithmetic from spendable cash

The fictional issue price is 20 per share. Multiplying the earlier 1,000 shares by 20 gives a 20,000 pre-issue implied value; multiplying 1,250 by 20 gives 25,000 after the issue. The difference is the 5,000 new investment in this deliberately simplified model. A valuation label is not a bank balance. Adding an option pool, different rights or other instruments changes the inputs rather than merely adding another stage name.

## Treat future instruments as their own contract

The [Y Combinator SAFE page](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe) explains post-money SAFE ownership estimates before new priced-round money and a separate optional pro-rata side letter. In a restricted illustration, 100,000 invested at a 2,000,000 post-money cap estimates 5% in that stated SAFE scope. That is not a promise of 5% after all future financing or a statement that issued shares already exist. Conversion, later dilution and rights require their actual document terms.

## Review the outcome instead of memorizing a ladder

Write issued versus future securities, denominator, transaction recipient, cash raised, assumed price and omitted rights. If a later round issues another 250 shares in the first model, the earlier investor’s unchanged 250 shares now represent 250/1,500, or one sixth. No stage label such as seed or IPO produces that fraction automatically. Complete the checkpoint using these fictional numbers; the lesson does not choose financing terms, reproduce a funding infographic or certify legal, tax or investment outcomes.
