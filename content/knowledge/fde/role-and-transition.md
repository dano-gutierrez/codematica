A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE, sometimes FDSE) works close to customer teams to turn an operational problem into working software and sustained use. You still write and review code. You also discover requirements, negotiate scope, integrate awkward systems, explain tradeoffs and transfer ownership. A successful demo is one milestone; an adopted, supportable workflow is the outcome.

The linked OpenAI and Palantir postings illustrate this combination of customer work and engineering. They are dated examples, not a universal job definition. Travel, seniority, on-call, coding depth and commercial responsibilities vary. Ask the hiring team for an example deployment and who owns each phase.

## Choose the role you actually want

| Role shape | Typical emphasis to investigate | Evidence to request from the team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product/platform FDE | Customer applications and reusable platform improvements | Recent field work that changed the product |
| Applied AI FDE | Workflow integration, retrieval, evaluation and bounded model actions | Production evaluation and human escalation process |
| Data/industrial FDE | Data integration, domain models and operational decisions | Data quality ownership and users taking action |
| Solutions/sales engineer | Often technical evaluation and presales | Whether you ship and maintain production code |
| Implementation/consulting engineer | Often integration and delivery against a scope | Who owns architecture, ongoing support and reuse |

These categories overlap. Titles alone do not establish responsibility, quota, compensation or technical depth.

## Build a bridge from your background

| Starting point | Transferable strength | Practice first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backend | APIs, state, reliability | Discovery, a usable interface, adoption measurement |
| Frontend and mobile | User workflows, feedback, interaction | SQL, connectors, permissions and deployment |
| Data engineering/analytics | Messy data, lineage, measurement | Transactional APIs, application UX and support |
| ML/research | Experiments, uncertainty, evaluation | Shipping full workflows, access control and handover |
| SRE/platform | Operations, observability, infrastructure | Domain discovery, scoping and user-facing prototypes |
| QA/test engineering | Failure analysis and reproducibility | Implementing a vertical slice and leading decisions |
| Embedded/industrial | Physical constraints and domain reasoning | Web/cloud integration, identity and enterprise data |
| Solutions/consulting | Stakeholders and domain fluency | Independently coding, testing and owning failures |

You need working depth in one programming language, SQL, HTTP/JSON, Git, automated tests and one deployment environment. You need enough breadth to debug the path from a user action through an API, datastore, queue and dependency. Learn a second language only when a target role or project needs it.

## Follow an evidence-based schedule

Use twelve weeks at roughly eight to ten hours per week as a planning example, not a hiring guarantee. Weeks 1–2 cover role fit, discovery, value and data. Weeks 3–4 build integrations and security. Weeks 5–6 cover AI and evaluation. Weeks 7–8 focus on production, incidents, adoption and communication. Week 9 analyzes cases; weeks 10–11 build and test the capstone; week 12 rehearses interviews and prepares a launch plan. Extend the schedule for unfamiliar fundamentals. Read the whole path even if your target role is not AI-focused, then spend extra lab time on its dominant technical needs.

## Practice

Score each capability from 0 (cannot explain), 1 (can explain), 2 (can demonstrate with help), to 3 (can deliver and defend independently). Attach a repository, diagram, test output or factual story to each score. Choose your two largest gaps and book two weekly practice sessions. Start the portfolio repository now; add evidence after every unit.

## Review your work

Can you describe a customer outcome, the code you would own and the evidence of adoption? Can you explain your strongest transferable skill without inventing customer experience? A completed reading path or quiz is curriculum progress; it does not verify delivery competence or certify job readiness.
