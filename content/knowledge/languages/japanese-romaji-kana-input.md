## The Short Answer

`こんばんは` is pronounced and learner-romanized **konbanwa**, but a Japanese input method needs **konbanha** to produce the written [は](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ha).

Nothing in the greeting changed from `wa` to `ha`. You are looking at two different jobs:

- **Japanese spelling:** `こんばんは` tells you what is written.
- **Pronunciation:** `こんばんわ` approximates what it sounds like.
- **Learner romaji:** `konbanwa` tells you how to read it.
- **IME keys:** `konbanha` tells the keyboard how to produce the spelling `こんばんは`.

An IME converts key sequences into kana. It is not judging the best way to romanize a Japanese sentence for a reader.

## Why The Greeting Ends In は

The older, fuller expression began with **今晩は…**: “As for this evening…”. The rest of the sentence was eventually omitted, but the topic particle [は](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ha) remained.

- [今](/languages/japanese/characters/kanji/now) = now or this
- [晩](/languages/japanese/characters/kanji/evening) = evening
- [は](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ha) = the topic particle, pronounced **wa**

The same pattern explains `こんにちは`, historically connected with **今日は…**, “As for today…”.

## The Three Particle Exceptions

When these kana are ordinary parts of words, use their normal sound. When they are particles, their pronunciation changes.

- [は](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ha) as a particle is pronounced `わ`, romanized `wa`, and typed `ha`. In the ordinary word `はな` (`hana`, flower), it keeps the sound `ha`.
- [へ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/he) as a particle is pronounced `え`, romanized `e`, and typed `he`. In `へや` (`heya`, room), it keeps the sound `he`.
- [を](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/wo) is pronounced and romanized `o`, but typed `wo`. Beginners will see it almost entirely as a particle.

The topic particle `は` marks what the sentence is about. The direction particle `へ` points toward a destination. The object particle `を` marks what an action affects.

## Read, Understand, Then Type

These examples keep learner romaji and IME input separate. Spaces make the grammar visible; an IME can usually accept the same keys without spaces.

1. **今晩は。 / こんばんは。** Read `konbanwa`; type `konbanha`. 今晩 means “this evening,” and は is the topic particle.
2. **今日は。 / こんにちは。** Read `konnichiwa`; type `konnichiha`. 今日 means “today,” and は is the topic particle.
3. **わたしは日本人です。 / わたしはにほんじんです。** Read `watashi wa nihonjin desu`; type `watashiha nihonjin desu`. わたし is “I,” and 日本人 is “Japanese person.”
4. **これは本です。 / これはほんです。** Read `kore wa hon desu`; type `koreha hon desu`. これ is “this,” and 本 is “book.”
5. **山は大きいです。 / やまはおおきいです。** Read `yama wa ookii desu`; type `yamaha ookii desu`. 山 is “mountain,” and 大きい is “big.”
6. **はなはきれいです。** Read `hana wa kirei desu`; type `hanaha kirei desu`. The first は is `ha` inside はな; the second is particle `wa`.
7. **はははげんきです。** Read `haha wa genki desu`; type `hahaha genki desu`. はは means “mother”; the final は is the topic particle.
8. **日本へいきます。 / にほんへいきます。** Read `nihon e ikimasu`; type `nihonhe ikimasu`. 日本 is Japan, へ means “toward,” and いきます means “go.”
9. **山へいきます。 / やまへいきます。** Read `yama e ikimasu`; type `yamahe ikimasu`. The destination is the mountain.
10. **へやへかえります。** Read `heya e kaerimasu`; type `heyahe kaerimasu`. The first へ is `he` in へや; the second is particle `e`.
11. **水をのみます。 / みずをのみます。** Read `mizu o nomimasu`; type `mizuwo nomimasu`. 水 is water, を marks the object, and のみます means “drink.”
12. **本をよみます。 / ほんをよみます。** Read `hon o yomimasu`; type `honwo yomimasu`. 本 is a book, and よみます means “read.”
13. **これをください。** Read `kore o kudasai`; type `korewo kudasai`. It means “Please give me this.”
14. **がっこう.** Read `gakkoo` or `gakkō`; type `gakkou`. Small [っ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-tsu) doubles the next consonant, while こう supplies the long o sound.
15. **きょう.** Read `kyoo` or `kyō`; type `kyou`. [き](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ki) plus small [ょ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-yo) makes `kyo`, and う lengthens the o sound.
16. **おおきい.** Read `ookii` or `ōkii`; type `ookii`. The full input shows both long-vowel spellings.
17. **せんぱい.** Read and type `senpai`; it may sound closer to `sempai`. The kana remains [ん](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/n).
18. **れんあい.** Read `ren'ai`; commonly type `rennai`. The second n commits ん before the following vowel.

## Hepburn Display And IME Aliases

Hepburn-style romaji helps an English-speaking beginner approximate the sound. Many IMEs also accept systematic aliases that follow the kana grid.

- [し](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/shi): learner romaji `shi`; IME aliases `shi` and `si`.
- [ち](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/chi): learner romaji `chi`; IME aliases `chi` and `ti`.
- [つ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/tsu): learner romaji `tsu`; IME aliases `tsu` and `tu`.
- [ふ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/fu): learner romaji `fu`; IME aliases `fu` and `hu`.
- [じ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ji): learner romaji `ji`; IME aliases `ji` and `zi`.

Use the learner romaji when explaining pronunciation. Use whichever accepted IME sequence reliably creates the intended kana when typing.

## A Beginner Routine

1. Read the Japanese spelling first.
2. Identify whether は, へ, or を is a particle.
3. Say the learner romaji aloud: wa, e, or o for those particles.
4. When typing kana, enter ha, he, or wo so the IME creates the written particle.
5. Tap any linked character above to inspect its reading, meaning, examples, stroke order, and writing practice.
