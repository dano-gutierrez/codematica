## Your First Reading System

Hiragana is not an alphabet with separate consonant and vowel letters. Most symbols represent one **mora**, a steady timing unit such as `か` (`ka`) or `み` (`mi`). Learn the sound and shape together, then recall it before revealing the answer.

The Japan Foundation's IRODORI Starter course introduces hiragana before katakana. That is our order too: hiragana appears constantly in grammar, word endings, and beginner vocabulary.

## Five Sounds That Break The Simple Pattern

The grid is a memory aid, not a command to pronounce every cell mechanically.

- `し` is **shi**, not “si.”
- `ち` is **chi**, not “ti.”
- `つ` is **tsu**, not “tu.”
- `ふ` is a light **fu** sound, made without strongly biting the lower lip.
- `を` is normally pronounced **o** when it marks an object.

Your keyboard may accept systematic aliases such as `si`, `ti`, `tu`, and `hu`, but learner romaji shows the usual sound-oriented forms.

## Learn In Small Rows

Use a three-pass loop for each group of five or ten:

1. **Notice:** open each character and compare its printed shape, sound, and stroke start points.
2. **Recall:** use flashcards and say the sound before reading the explanation.
3. **Use:** read a short word containing only kana you have learned, then trace or write the characters once.

Writing helps you notice construction, but recognition comes first. Do not wait for perfect handwriting before reading real words.

## Look-Alikes Worth Comparing

- `あ` and `お`: `お` has a small mark at the upper right.
- `き` and `さ`: `き` has two horizontal crossings; `さ` has one.
- `ぬ`, `め`, and `ね`: look for the loop and where the final tail ends.
- `れ`, `わ`, and `ね`: they share a left stem, so inspect the right side.
- `る` and `ろ`: `る` closes with a small loop; `ろ` stays open.

Compare confusing characters side by side, then mix them into the full deck. That is more useful than copying one symbol many times without recall.

## First Reading Challenge

Try these without romaji first:

- あさ — morning
- いぬ — dog
- ねこ — cat
- すし — sushi
- はな — flower
- やま — mountain
- かわ — river
- ほん — book

Slow, correct recall is enough. Reading speed grows through repeated use.
