# Japanese First Connections

Learn each pattern as something a person is doing. Read the scene aloud, notice the small grammar word, then change one detail to make the sentence yours.

## Scene 1: meeting someone

> 👋 はじめまして。アナです。アメリカじんです。よろしくおねがいします。
>
> *Hajimemashite. Ana desu. Amerika-jin desu. Yoroshiku onegai shimasu.*
>
> Nice to meet you. I’m Ana. I’m American. It’s a pleasure to meet you.

`です` politely identifies or describes something. Japanese often leaves out “I” when the situation makes it obvious.

- わたしはアナです。 — *Watashi wa Ana desu.* — I am Ana.
- わたしもがくせいです。 — *Watashi mo gakusei desu.* — I am also a student.
- マリアさんはせんせいですか。 — *Maria-san wa sensei desu ka?* — Is Maria a teacher?

`は` marks the topic and is pronounced **wa** here. `も` means “also.” Sentence-final `か` makes a polite question.

Try it: say your name, then add one true fact such as your country, language, work, or student status.

## Scene 2: identifying things

> 📚 A: これはなんですか。
>
> B: それはにほんごのほんです。
>
> A: だれのほんですか。
>
> B: わたしのほんです。

- `これ` — this, near the speaker
- `それ` — that, near the listener
- `あれ` — that over there, away from both
- `どれ` — which one
- `の` — connects nouns; often possession or category

`にほんごのほん` is literally “a Japanese-language book.” Tap the words in the dictionary when you need the reading; do not wait to understand every kanji before reading the scene.

## Numbers, ages, time, and dates

Count with the useful form required by the task.

| Task | Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| say your age | じゅうろくさいです | jūroku-sai desu | I am 16 years old |
| ask the time | いま、なんじですか | ima, nan-ji desu ka | What time is it now? |
| answer the time | くじはんです | ku-ji han desu | It is 9:30 |
| say a date | はちがつよっかです | hachi-gatsu yokka desu | It is August 4 |

Some counters have special readings. Learn them in useful chunks: `ひとり` one person, `ふたり` two people, `ひとつ` one thing, and `ふたつ` two things.

## Family and friends

Use the plain family word for your own family when speaking to someone outside it.

- ちちはかいしゃいんです。 — My father is a company employee.
- はははパンがすきです。 — My mother likes bread.
- ともだちもにほんごをべんきょうします。 — My friend also studies Japanese.

The repeated `ははは` is not a typo: `はは` means “my mother,” followed by the topic particle `は` pronounced *wa*.

## Likes, food, drinks, and ordering

> ☕ A: なにがすきですか。
>
> B: コーヒーがすきです。でも、おちゃもすきです。

> 🍜 A: ラーメンをひとつ、おねがいします。
>
> B: はい。おみずもどうぞ。

`がすきです` expresses what someone likes. For a simple order, `[item] + counter + おねがいします` is useful and polite.

## Mini-reader: あさのカフェ (Level Start)

> 🌅 あさです。アナさんはカフェにいます。
>
> ☕ 「コーヒーをひとつ、おねがいします。」
>
> 🥐 パンもあります。アナさんはパンがすきです。
>
> 😊 「おいしいです。」

**Reading:** Asa desu. Ana-san wa kafe ni imasu. “Kōhī o hitotsu, onegai shimasu.” Pan mo arimasu. Ana-san wa pan ga suki desu. “Oishii desu.”

**Meaning:** It is morning. Ana is at a café. “One coffee, please.” There is bread too. Ana likes bread. “It’s delicious.”

After reading, cover the English and answer: Where is Ana? What does she order? What does she like?

## Can-do self-check

- I can exchange a greeting and give my name.
- I can ask and answer a basic identity question.
- I can identify a nearby object and ask whose it is.
- I can understand a simple age, time, or date.
- I can say one family fact and one preference.
- I can make a simple food or drink order.

These are practical A1 targets, not a promise of JLPT or JFT-Basic performance. Use the checkpoint to find what to review.
