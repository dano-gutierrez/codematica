## A Few Marks Unlock Many Sounds

You do not need to memorize a second large chart. Start from kana you already know and notice the small change.

## Dakuten: The Two Little Marks

Dakuten `゛` voices a consonant. Feel the difference by touching your throat: `か` is quiet in the throat; `が` vibrates.

| Base | Voiced row | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| k → g | が ぎ ぐ げ ご | `か` ka → [が](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ga) ga |
| s → z / j | ざ じ ず ぜ ぞ | `し` shi → [じ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ji) ji |
| t → d / j / z | だ ぢ づ で ど | `た` ta → だ da |
| h → b | ば び ぶ べ ぼ | `は` ha → [ば](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/ba) ba |

## Handakuten: The Small Circle

The circle `゜` changes the h-row to p: [ぱ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/pa) `pa`, ぴ `pi`, ぷ `pu`, ぺ `pe`, ぽ `po`.

## Small っ: Hold Before The Next Consonant

[っ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-tsu) is smaller than `つ`. It creates a brief closure before the following consonant.

- きて `kite` — come
- きって `kitte` — stamp
- さか `saka` — slope
- さっか `sakka` — author

When typing, double the next consonant: `kitte`, `sakka`, `gakkou`.

## Small ゃ, ゅ, ょ: One Contracted Beat

Put small [ゃ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-ya), [ゅ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-yu), or [ょ](/languages/japanese/characters/hiragana/small-yo) after an i-row kana.

- き + ゃ → きゃ `kya`
- し + ゅ → しゅ `shu`
- ち + ょ → ちょ `cho`
- に + ゅ → にゅ `nyu`
- り + ょ → りょ `ryo`

Size changes the rhythm: `きや` is `ki-ya`, while `きゃ` is the single contracted mora `kya`.

## Long Vowels In Hiragana

Hiragana often writes long vowels with another vowel kana.

- おばさん `obasan` — aunt
- おばあさん `obaasan` — grandmother
- ここ `koko` — here
- こうこう `koukou` — high school
- せんせい `sensei` — teacher; the `ei` often sounds like a long e

Do not erase the written vowel just because a romanization uses a macron such as `ō` or `ē`. The kana spelling is what the IME needs.

## Read The Size And Marks

Before guessing a word, ask:

1. Is there dakuten or handakuten?
2. Is the kana full size or small?
3. Does small っ hold the next consonant?
4. Does the following vowel extend the sound?

This visual checklist prevents most early decoding mistakes.
