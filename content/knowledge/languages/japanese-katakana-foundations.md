## Same Sounds, New Shapes

Katakana represents the same basic morae as hiragana. `カ` and `か` are both `ka`; the script changes, not the sound map.

Katakana is common in loanwords, foreign names, sound effects, scientific names, and emphasis. The Japan Foundation gives examples such as メール (mail), プレゼント (present), and ハンバーガー (hamburger).

## The Long Vowel Mark ー

[ー](/languages/japanese/characters/katakana/long-vowel-mark) extends the vowel immediately before it.

- [コーヒー](/languages/japanese/vocabulary/coffee) `koohii` — coffee
- [タクシー](/languages/japanese/vocabulary/taxi) `takushii` — taxi
- [スーパー](/languages/japanese/vocabulary/supermarket) `suupaa` — supermarket

The dash is sound, not punctuation. In horizontal writing it is horizontal; in traditional vertical writing it turns vertical.

## The Four Famous Look-Alikes

Compare stroke direction and placement, not only the final silhouette.

- `シ` shi: the short strokes sit more horizontally; the long stroke rises from lower left.
- `ツ` tsu: the short strokes stand more vertically; the long stroke falls from upper right.
- `ソ` so: one short stroke, then a falling long stroke.
- `ン` n: one short stroke, then a long stroke that rises from lower left.

Also compare `ク` ku / `ケ` ke, `チ` chi / `テ` te, `ワ` wa / `ウ` u, and `ヌ` nu / `ス` su.

## Read Useful Words

Loanwords adapt to Japanese sound patterns, so recognize the kana rather than forcing the original English spelling.

- テレビ `terebi` — television
- ホテル `hoteru` — hotel
- レストラン `resutoran` — restaurant
- アメリカ `amerika` — America / the United States
- コンピューター `konpyuutaa` — computer

## A Reliable Practice Loop

1. Learn one row while keeping the hiragana sounds nearby.
2. Recall the sound from the katakana shape.
3. Write each symbol once with the numbered stroke guide.
4. Read two real loanwords.
5. Mix the row into the full flashcard deck on a later session.
